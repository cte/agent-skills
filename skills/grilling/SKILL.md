# Stress-test a plan

Map the proposal as a decision tree: goals and constraints first, then decisions
whose answers unlock narrower design choices. Read supplied material before
asking the user to repeat it.

Ask about the current frontier: decisions whose prerequisites are already known.
Use a small round of questions with your recommendation and its tradeoff. Honor
the user's preferred cadence, including one question at a time. Questions that
depend on an unanswered choice belong in a later round.

Facts are yours to investigate with available read-only tools. Decisions are the
user's to make. Do not ask for information already present in code, documents, or
the conversation. If independent exploration is permitted and useful it may run
in parallel; delegation is optional, not a prerequisite for this skill.

After each answer, update settled choices, unresolved assumptions, consequences,
and the next frontier. Challenge inconsistencies directly with evidence and
concrete alternatives. Do not invent objections just to extend the interview.

Finish when the material decisions are settled or the user ends the exercise.
Summarize decisions, deliberate assumptions, and remaining risks. Do not treat a
design discussion as authorization to implement or publish; follow any explicit
implementation request already given.
